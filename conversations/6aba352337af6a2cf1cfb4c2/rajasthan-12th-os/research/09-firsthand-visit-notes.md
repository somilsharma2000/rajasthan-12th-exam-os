# First-Hand Site Visit Notes — Testbook.com
*Agent personally visited testbook.com in a live browser session, 2026-09-30. Primary-source observations only (what I actually saw on the pages). Deeper page-by-page audits running as separate missions.*

## Homepage observations (verbatim structure)
1. Positioning line: "One Destination for Complete Exam Preparation" with the loop "Learn → Practice → Improve → Succeed"
2. Hook: "Start your preparation for selections. For Free!" — free-first entry, signup framed as free
3. Social proof counters (massive scale): "9+ Crore Registered Students", "4+ Lacs Student Selections", "242+ Crore Tests Attempted", "5.5+ Crore Classes Attended" — a new app cannot fake this scale; our trust model must be transparency (verified counts, sources shown) instead of inflated scale
4. Navigation: Exams / SuperCoaching / Test Series / Books / Pass — "Exams" is a first-class menu; every exam gets its own page (validates our Exam Hub design)
5. Pass value pitch: "All India Rank, Latest Exam Patterns, In-depth Performance Analysis, Multi-lingual Mock Tests"
6. Supercoaching: courses branded around "India's Super Teachers"; Hindi batch names with emotional punch: "रफ़्तार", "जुनून", "अंतिम प्रहार" — proof that Hindi emotional naming converts; capture naming style for our test series
7. Free live classes section = YouTube-style funnel converted on-platform ("LIVE NOW", "Remind Me" buttons)
8. Sale banner (site-wide): "Shubh Aarambh Sale is Live, Get Yearly Pass at ₹399 Grab Now" — festival-sale pricing psychology; ₹399 is their annual anchor price; our ₹199-249 undercuts it with honest positioning
9. Support funnel: "Got any Queries? Quickly chat with our Experts on WhatsApp!" — WhatsApp as sales channel (same channel our audience lives on; we can use it for support, honestly)
10. "Refer & Earn" exists as a referral product — validated demand for referral mechanics

## Notable weakness found first-hand
- Broken deep links: two state-exam URLs I tried returned their 404 page ("Oops! Couldn't find this page"). Even the market leader's Rajasthan pages are fragile — a Rajasthan-specialist app where every exam page actually works is a real differentiator, not a nice-to-have.

## What we adopt vs avoid (feeding the synthesis later)
- ADOPT: free-first entry, exams-as-first-class pages, Hindi emotional naming for test series, WhatsApp as support channel, referral mechanic (post-validation)
- AVOID: fake-feeling scale counters for a new product, cluttered sale banners, dark-pattern urgency ("Grab Now" pop-ups on every page)
- OUR ANGLE: trust through verification transparency (every question shows source + verification status — none of them show this)
