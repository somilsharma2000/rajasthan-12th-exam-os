# USER_JOURNEYS.md — The Student's Life Inside the App
*UX Research + Product departments, 2026-09-30. Founder asked: fully analyze the student profile and how the app makes him improve and learn.*

## 1. WHO HE IS (student persona, evidence-based)
- 18-23, Hindi-medium, 12th pass, small-town/district Rajasthan (Sikar, Jhunjhunu, Barmer, Bharatpur type)
- Family pressure + limited money: budget ₹50-500 total, UPI pocket money; often first in family attempting a government job
- Studies 4-8 hours/day, phone is his only computer; network often 2G/3G
- Today he learns free on YouTube, buys ₹50 Telegram PDFs, and has NO timed practice, NO error tracking, NO feedback loop
- Fears: scams, wrong answer keys, wasted years between notifications
- Emotional reality: motivation swings, exam dates get postponed, he needs proof he's improving or he quits

## 2. HIS LIFE INSIDE THE APP (the journey)
**Stage 1 — Orientation (first 5 minutes)**
Selects his exam → sees the Exam Hub (pattern, syllabus, selection stages, official links — trust established because everything shows its official source) → Hindi-first, no signup wall.

**Stage 2 — Baseline (first 30 minutes)**
Takes a short diagnostic (25-35 questions per subject). Result: his *Exam DNA* — accuracy, speed, guessing pattern, strong/weak topics. He sees, often for the first time in his life, an honest map of where he stands.

**Stage 3 — The Daily Loop (his everyday life)**
Opens app → "What should I do now?" one screen: today's plan with reasoning ("Your LCM questions took 2.1 min avg — 10 speed drills today") → does it → sees progress bar move → streak grows honestly.

**Stage 4 — The Learning Cycle (how he actually learns)**
LEARN (topic sheet: concept → intuition → example → common trap) → PRACTICE (untimed, explanation after every answer + AI coach for doubts) → TEST (timed, negative marking, exam pressure) → ANALYZE (error book: every mistake with root cause) → RECOVER (auto drills generated from his exact mistakes) → RETAIN (1/3/7/15/30-day spaced revision) → SIMULATE (full mock, exam feel).

**Stage 5 — Improvement Proof (why he stays)**
Readiness score per exam trending up; accuracy per topic visible; mock deep-analysis shows "negative marking cost you 7 marks — here are the 9 questions you should have left". Improvement he can SEE is the retention engine.

**Stage 6 — Notification Drops (sprint mode)**
Exam calendar alert → app shifts his plan: application window checklist, revision-first schedule, mock cadence increase. (This is when competitors' marketing strikes — our app already knows him.)

**Stage 7 — Result (either way)**
Selected → done. Not selected → error book + Exam DNA carry over to the next exam in the same app (CET family) — his preparation compounds instead of restarting. THIS is the honest moat: the app remembers him.

## 3. HOW THE APP MAKES HIM IMPROVE (the machinery)
1. Every answer feeds the system: correct/wrong, time taken, guess vs known (5th-option rule usage)
2. Root-cause classification: concept gap / calculation slip / misread / time pressure / blind guess → different drills for each
3. Weakness-targeted content: the app serves his weakest micro-topic first, not a fixed syllabus order
4. Spaced repetition: correct-once is not mastered; the system re-tests at 1/3/7/15/30 days
5. Adaptive difficulty: starts where he is, rises as he proves himself (no demotivating walls)
6. AI coach (approved D-009): 24/7 Hindi doubt solving, labeled AI, constrained to syllabus, falls back to verified explanations
7. Honest motivation: streaks + milestones + readiness trend, no fake badges, no dark patterns
8. Gap-time protection: between notifications, evergreen PYQ bank + GK core keep the habit alive

## 4. FAILURE MODES TO DESIGN AGAINST (adversarial)
1. First-session overwhelm → orientation must be one exam, one diagnostic, one plan (nothing else visible)
2. Diagnostic fear → framed as "map", never "test/judge"
3. Motivation crash after bad mock → deep-analysis always ends with "here is exactly what to fix this week"
4. Guess-spamming → guessing pattern tracked and called out kindly
5. Streak-break rage → honest forgiveness mechanics, no guilt traps
6. Multi-exam confusion → one active exam focus, others parked
